# Expectation Retrospectives — Meta-Expectations for the Process, Not the Outcome

**Thesis (captain, near-verbatim intent):** part of learning-to-learn is a
retrospective, first-principles look at anything that did not meet
expectations. Prerequisite: define **meta-expectations for the workflow and
process itself** — not the outcome or the charter's DoD, but the meta-process:
*How should this work feel? What does success look like? What would failure
look like? When should we finish?* Then: if anything misses those
expectations — **even when the outcome succeeded** — that miss is itself a
learning object: not a correction to the work, but to the process that
executed the work. In human terms: HR- and sprint-review-class meetings, not
just code review.

## Why (captain, 2026-07-19 — the parent principle)

> Collaboration works best with an established relationship of trust. Trust
> is built and maintained by managing expectations. This is the
> meta-expectations management to build and maintain trust in order to
> establish a collaborative relationship.

The rubric and the retro are not process hygiene — they are **trust
instruments**. Declared expectations are promises with a shape; the retro is
the audit that keeps the promises honest; each kept (or honestly-missed and
repaired) expectation compounds into the trust that lets delegation widen.
The causal chain runs: expectation management → trust → collaboration —
and every existing mechanism (attestations, receipts, verdict-first reports,
the ratification ledger, the creed itself) is this same chain in different
clothing: each one is a promise about claims, kept auditable.

## What this adds that the existing machinery lacks

Outcome validation (G5, DoD) answers *did we build the right thing right*.
The debrief's candidate lines answer *what did the failures teach*. Neither
instrument catches **a success that felt wrong** — grinding, opaque, poorly
communicated, finished late or never-quite-finished — and those misses are
where process debt hides, because nothing red ever appears.

## The instrument (v0, captain's first stab — refine over time)

Declared per process/charter as a short rubric, retro'd at debrief:

- **Empowering** — the operator (human or seat) had the authority and tools
  the work needed; escalations were decisions, not permissions.
- **Productive / efficient** — effort landed on the work, not the workaround;
  casualties bought lessons, not repetition.
- **Well planned** — the charter's shape survived contact; replans were
  learnings, not surprises.
- **Well executed** — the lane held; discipline wasn't heroics.
- **Well validated** — fresh eyes gated every landing; verdicts were earned.
- **Directions and policies followed** — including communication: the captain
  informed at the right altitude, interrupted only for completions, blocks,
  and decisions.
- **Bounded** — "when should we finish" was known going in, and the finish
  matched it (or the miss was named, not drifted past).

**Retro protocol:** at debrief, walk the rubric expectation-vs-experience —
including on successes. Any miss gets first-principles treatment (what
expectation, what actually happened, what's the smallest process/law change
that closes the gap) and becomes a candidate line. A retro that only
inspects outcomes is a demo, not a retrospective.
