# Surprise-Is-the-Trigger, Crew-Is-the-Coverage

**Thesis:** unknown-unknowns are not caught by foresight — no amount of
upfront planning finds the blind spot you don't know you have. They are
caught by two things working together: expectation-deviation (something
felt surprising enough to say out loud) and non-overlapping vantages
(different roles noticing different classes of surprise because they're
looking from different angles).

## The pattern, observed four times in one shift

| Catch | Vantage | What made it visible |
|---|---|---|
| Time-drift ("tonight" used after dawn) | Captain | Noticed the mismatch between narration and observed `oddkit_time`, a check the seat was already running but not applying |
| Auth economics (D-OR6 vs D-OR2 never reconciled) | Captain | Recognized a category error — technical-axis labeling hiding an unreconciled economic axis |
| Evidence framing (single-incident flagged honestly) | Validator | Fresh-context read of the promotion draft caught unsupported generalization before merge |
| Stale line counts | Plan flight | Cross-checked a plan's cited figures against current repo state and found drift |
| D-OR2 vs Move 4 conflict | Seat | Independently surfaced the lane conflict the captain then ruled on |

No single vantage caught all four. Each role's blind spot was a different
role's obvious.

## The proposal

Treat "surprise" (a felt mismatch between expectation and observation) as
the trigger condition for escalation, and treat crew diversity — genuinely
different roles reading the same work from different angles — as the
coverage mechanism, not a redundancy cost to be trimmed. A single reviewer,
however careful, has one vantage; four catches in one shift came from four
different ones.

## Evidence

Four independent catches within a single shift (2026-07-19 evening →
2026-07-20 morning), across four distinct vantage points (captain ×2,
validator ×1, plan flight ×1, seat ×1), each catching a different class of
miss. This is same-shift evidence — flagged honestly as a narrower
independence bar than a multi-day pattern, though the four catches are
mechanistically distinct (not the same catch repeated).

## Open question for ratification

Whether this becomes a structural requirement (a minimum vantage count per
review) or remains a naming of an already-working pattern is not resolved
